# Marqui

> **Explore the real world. Discover its story.**

## Overview

**Marqui** is a location-based exploration and learning platform designed to turn real-world cultural and historical locations into interactive game experiences.

The concept combines **geolocation, gamification, artificial intelligence, and pixel-art game design** to encourage people to physically explore museums, monuments, historical sites, artworks, landmarks, and other culturally significant places.

Instead of simply showing users information about a location, Marqui gives them a reason to **visit, discover, learn, and interact with it**.

The core idea is:

```text
Explore → Discover → Learn → Challenge → Reward → Explore Again
```

## The Problem

Many museums, monuments, and cultural locations contain significant educational and historical information, but the traditional experience can be passive.

Visitors may:

- Walk past exhibits without engaging with them
- Read limited amounts of information
- Have difficulty maintaining interest
- Experience the location as a static collection of information
- Have little incentive to explore beyond the most obvious attractions

At the same time, people are already familiar with interactive experiences through mobile games and location-based applications.

Marqui aims to combine these two experiences.

## The Idea

Marqui treats the **real world as the game board**.

A user's surroundings become a map containing discoverable locations.

These locations could include:

- Museums
- Art museums
- Historical monuments
- Forts
- Heritage buildings
- Statues
- Murals
- Archaeological sites
- Cultural landmarks
- Scientific locations
- Natural landmarks
- Public artworks

Instead of simply displaying these locations on a map, Marqui turns them into interactive discoveries.

A user can approach a location, discover it, learn about it, complete challenges, and receive rewards for exploration.

## How It Works

The primary experience is built around geographical exploration.

A simplified user journey would be:

```text
Open Marqui
      ↓
Explore the map
      ↓
Find a nearby location
      ↓
Travel toward the location
      ↓
Discover it
      ↓
Learn its story
      ↓
Complete a challenge
      ↓
Earn XP / rewards
      ↓
Add it to your collection
      ↓
Continue exploring
```

The objective is to create a continuous connection between **physical movement and digital learning**.

## Museums as Game Worlds

Museums are an important part of the Marqui concept.

When a user enters a participating museum, the experience could transition from the outdoor map into a dedicated **pixel-art museum environment**.

The museum becomes a playable level.

Users could explore different rooms, discover exhibits, learn about them, answer questions, and complete museum-specific missions.

For example:

```text
World Map
    ↓
Museum discovered
    ↓
Museum entrance
    ↓
Pixel-art museum environment
    ↓
Explore rooms
    ↓
Discover exhibits
    ↓
Learn
    ↓
Complete quizzes
    ↓
Complete museum missions
    ↓
Earn rewards
```

This would transform a traditional museum visit into an interactive exploration experience.

## AI-Powered Learning

AI would act as a personalization layer within Marqui.

The system could adapt information and challenges based on factors such as:

- Age group
- Interests
- Previous discoveries
- Quiz performance
- Exploration history
- Difficulty preference

AI could assist with:

- Exhibit identification
- Educational explanations
- Quiz generation
- Mission generation
- Personalized recommendations
- Difficulty adaptation

AI-generated information would ideally be grounded in **trusted museum, historical, or curated data**.

## Gamification

Marqui uses game mechanics to make exploration rewarding.

### XP

Users gain experience by discovering locations, learning, completing quizzes, and finishing missions.

### Levels

XP contributes to player progression.

### Badges

Users can unlock achievements for different types of exploration.

Examples:

```text
Museum Explorer
History Hunter
Art Explorer
Fort Hunter
City Explorer
Cultural Explorer
```

### Missions

Missions provide users with specific exploration objectives.

For example:

> Discover three historical locations in your city.

or:

> Find two artworks created during the same period.

### Collections

Every discovery can become part of a user's personal collection, creating a digital record of the places they have explored.

## Pixel-Art Experience

Marqui is designed around a **pixel-art game aesthetic**.

The interface would combine real-world geographical information with visual elements inspired by classic pixel games and location-based games.

The goal is for the application to feel less like a conventional tourism or educational application and more like a **game world built around the real world**.

The map represents the player's surroundings, while museums and other significant locations can become dedicated pixel-art environments.

## The Core Philosophy

Marqui is not intended to encourage users to spend more time staring at their phones.

The phone is the **interface**, not the destination.

The fundamental idea is:

> **Use the screen to make people look up.**

The application provides information, challenges, and rewards, but the actual experience happens in the physical world.

```text
Phone
  ↓
Curiosity
  ↓
Exploration
  ↓
Real-world discovery
  ↓
Learning
```

## Long-Term Vision

The initial concept is centered around museums and cultural exploration, but the underlying idea can extend to an entire city or eventually the world.

A city could become a massive interactive learning environment.

A monument could become a quest.

A museum could become a game level.

An artwork could become a collectible.

A historical district could become a multi-step adventure.

The long-term vision is:

> **Make the world an interactive learning game.**
